This policy explains what personal data Engineer Booklet processes, why, on what legal basis, and what rights you have over it — including the specific rights granted under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the UK GDPR, the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR), and Switzerland's Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP).

Who we are

Engineer Booklet ("we," "us," "this site") operates engineer-booklet.com and the associated YouTube channel. It's run independently, without a separate registered business entity. For the purposes of GDPR and UK GDPR, Engineer Booklet is the data controller for the processing described in this policy. Every privacy matter — questions, corrections, removal requests, or anything else in this policy — goes to a single address: info@engineer-booklet.com.

What data we collect

The site itself has no accounts, no forms, and no server-side database — it's a static site, and it doesn't ask you for anything. Two things happen in the background regardless:

  • Browser storage we set. Your light/dark theme choice and your cookie-consent decision are saved in your browser's local storage. Neither is transmitted to us; we have no record of either.
  • Server access logs. Our hosting provider, Hostinger, automatically records standard technical data for every request — IP address, page requested, timestamp, user agent, and referrer — the way virtually every web host does, for security, abuse prevention, and keeping the site running.

Beyond that, the only other data processing happens through third parties you interact with directly — playing a YouTube embed, or (once live) seeing an ad — each covered in its own section below.

YouTube embeds

Video pages embed YouTube via youtube-nocookie.com, which limits cookies until you actually press play. Once you play a video, YouTube (Google) may set cookies and collect data under its own privacy policy. We don't receive or see any of that data ourselves.

Advertising (Google AdSense)

This site uses, or intends to use, Google AdSense to show ads. Google and its advertising partners use cookies to serve ads based on your prior visits to this and other sites. You can review or opt out of personalized advertising at Google Ads Settings, and read more at How Google uses information from sites that use its services.

Visitors in the EEA, UK, and Switzerland see the consent banner before any ad-related cookie is set, and can withdraw consent at any time.

Who we share data with

We don't sell personal data, and we don't have any of our own to share beyond what a third party collects directly through your own interaction with their embed. The processors and recipients involved are:

  • Hostinger — our hosting provider, processes server access logs.
  • Google (YouTube, AdSense) — processes data you generate by playing a video or being shown/interacting with an ad, as described above.

International data transfers

Google LLC is based in the United States. Where Google processes data described in this policy outside the EEA/UK/Switzerland, it does so under recognized safeguards — Google is a certified participant in the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and its UK and Swiss extensions, which the European Commission, the UK, and Switzerland recognize as providing an adequate level of protection. See Google's own privacy policy for their current certification and safeguards.

How long we keep data

We don't retain personal data ourselves — there's no database on our end to retain it in. Your theme and consent preferences stay in your browser's local storage until you clear your browser data or reset them yourself. Server access logs are retained by Hostinger under its own retention schedule; see Hostinger's privacy policy for specifics. Data held by Google (YouTube/AdSense) is retained under Google's own policies, which you can review or manage at Google's Data & Privacy controls.

Your rights

If GDPR, UK GDPR, or the Swiss FADP applies to you, you have the following rights over your personal data. Because we hold so little of it ourselves, most requests will point you to the relevant third party (Google) — but we'll always help you figure out where to go.

  • Access — ask what personal data we (or our processors) hold about you.
  • Rectification — ask us to correct inaccurate data.
  • Erasure / removal ("right to be forgotten") — ask us to delete data, where there's no overriding reason to keep it. Send removal requests to info@engineer-booklet.com.
  • Restriction — ask us to limit how we use your data in certain circumstances.
  • Portability — ask for data you've provided in a structured, machine-readable format.
  • Objection — object to processing based on legitimate interests, including at any time to any processing for advertising purposes.
  • Withdraw consent — at any time, with no effect on the lawfulness of processing before the withdrawal.
  • Not be subject to solely automated decisions — not relevant here: we don't use automated decision-making or profiling that produces legal or similarly significant effects.

Exercising your rights

Every request — removal, access, correction, objection, or anything else above — goes to the same place: email info@engineer-booklet.com and describe what you'd like. We'll respond within one month, as GDPR Article 12 requires (extendable by two further months for complex requests, in which case we'll tell you why). To withdraw cookie consent specifically, clear this site's local storage (or your browser's site data for engineer-booklet.com) and reload the page; the consent banner will ask again. To control what Google does with data it holds, use Google's own privacy controls directly, since we can't act on Google's data on your behalf.

Complaints

We'd rather you raised a concern with us directly first, at info@engineer-booklet.com, so we can try to put it right. You're also entitled to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority:

  • EEA: your local data protection authority — see the EDPB's list of national authorities.
  • UK: the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO).
  • Switzerland: the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC).

Children's privacy

This site is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from children under 16 — or the age of digital consent set by your own country's law, which under GDPR Article 8 may be as low as 13. If you believe a child has provided us data, contact us and we'll delete it.

Do Not Track & Global Privacy Control

Browser "Do Not Track" signals were never standardized around a common meaning, so this site doesn't respond to them. We do honor the Global Privacy Control (GPC) signal where your browser sends one: if detected, we treat it as an automatic opt-out of advertising cookies, equivalent to declining the consent banner, without showing the banner itself.

Changes to this policy
